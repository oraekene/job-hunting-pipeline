job</h2><div class="relative"><div class="styles_description__xjvTf inline-block text-black" id="job-description"><p><strong>About LawnStarter</strong></p>

<p>LawnStarter is the nation&#39;s leading on-demand marketplace for lawn care and outdoor services, with over $750M in bookings. We&#39;re expanding beyond lawn care to become the one-stop shop for home services: three brands (LawnStarter, Lawn Love, Home Gnome), one shared platform.</p>

<p><strong>About Product at LawnStarter</strong></p>

<p>Our BHAG: 1+ job per household, every year. Getting there means winning on four fronts at once: more customers, more Pros, excellent service experience for both sides, and an AI-run company that moves faster than its size should allow. We&#39;ve organized around those bets as swim lanes: New Customer Acquisition, Strengthen Supply, Service Experience, and AI &amp; Operating Excellence.</p>

<p><strong>The Role</strong></p>

<p>This is the most senior individual contributor seat in Product, with no direct reports. You&#39;ll work as a peer to the leaders of Ops, Data, Design, and Engineering.</p>

<p>Senior Product Managers here each own a workstream inside one of four swim lanes. You drive the Service Experience product agenda end to end: everything after a customer books, for customers and Pros, and the AI increasingly behind it. Two jobs at once: a critical thought partner to leadership on how AI reshapes our service experience, and an operator who ships with peers in engineering, design, data, marketing, and ops at a speed and quality that most would not believe. You&#39;re accountable for company-level numbers, not initiative-level ones.</p>

<p><strong>What makes this role different:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Thought partner, not order-taker.</strong> Leadership wants your view on where AI takes the service experience, and expects you to change their minds sometimes.</li>
<li><strong>Speed and quality together.</strong> Small team, peers in engineering, design, data, marketing, and ops who move fast, and few layers between your judgment and what ships.</li>
<li><strong>Ultra AI-forward is the point.</strong> We want someone already operating the way most PMs will operate in five years.</li>
<li><strong>Strategy and hands.</strong> You still prototype with LLMs, write eval sets, read transcripts, and re-architect live systems yourself.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Requirements</strong></p>

<p><strong>What You&#39;ll Own</strong></p>

<ul>
<li><strong>The Service Experience swim lane&#39;s product strategy and roadmap</strong>: marketplace trust after booking, including setting the right expectations on both sides, steering Pros toward great work, and the increasingly AI-assisted policies that arbitrate conflicts.</li>
<li><strong>AI-powered support for customers and Pros</strong>, including the eval systems that gate every change (golden datasets, automated judges, regression detection, human-review sampling).</li>
<li><strong>Two company-level numbers</strong>: Contribution LTV (profit per customer over their lifetime) and the brand-promise metrics.</li>
<li><strong>The vision and proof of concept for how AI transforms the customer and Pro experience</strong>, not just support.</li>
<li><strong>The bar for product</strong>: standards for shipping trustworthy AI, influencing Senior PMs by showing what great looks like, and being the person leadership calls for the hardest two-sided judgment calls.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Problems to Solve</strong></p>

<p><strong>Making AI support provably good, then letting it do a lot more.</strong> AI handles roughly a third of customer support conversations today. The goal for the next year: over half of all inquiries, customer and Pro, resolved with high satisfaction. Getting there is gated on one hard, ongoing, technical thing: proving the AI resolved a case rather than just closed it. That means defining &quot;good&quot; for an open-ended conversation, and catching quality drift before a customer or Pro feels it. You&#39;ll build Pro-side support on that foundation: unglamorous work that makes a slick demo safe to scale.</p>

<p><strong>Keeping the promise on both sides.</strong> Most service failures aren&#39;t bad work, they&#39;re mismatched expectations: the grass grew a tier past what was booked, or the customer wants a date the Pro can&#39;t commit to. Our promise is priced as quoted, done right, and keeping it means getting expectations right before the work starts and steering Pros toward great work. These aren&#39;t bad actors: both sides are right from where they sit, and the marketplace has to make a call. You&#39;ll build the policies and the increasingly AI-assisted systems that decide fairly and consistently at scale, and make the judgment call yourself when there&#39;s no clean answer.</p>

<p><strong>Making every customer more profitable.</strong> Trust isn&#39;t a soft metric here: customers who get what they were promised stay for years, and conflicts resolved fairly cost less to serve. You&#39;ll own Contribution LTV, targeting +10% year over year. The hard part is knowing which lever moves it (retention, cost to serve, fewer make-goods) and proving the attribution, not just asserting it.</p>

<p><strong>Deciding what AI does to the whole experience, not just support.</strong> AI already runs quietly in pricing, fraud detection, and routing. The next unlock is AI that customers and Pros notice. By year one, we want a working proof of concept and a leadership-endorsed vision for how AI transforms that experience. What&#39;s first? How do you know?</p>

<p><strong>What Success Looks Like (Year 1)</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>50%+ of support inquiries resolved by AI with high customer and Pro satisfaction, behind an eval suite blocking regressions, plus a first version of Pro-side AI support live.</li>
<li>Contribution LTV up 10% year over year, with attribution you can defend.</li>
<li>Brand-promise metric goals hit: priced as quoted, done right.</li>
<li>A working proof of concept and a leadership-endorsed vision for how AI transforms the customer and Pro experience.</li>
<li>Leadership treats you as the person who defines where the service experience goes, not just delivers it, and the team ships faster and better because you&#39;re in the room.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Who You Are</strong></p>

<p><strong>A track record of extraordinary outcomes.</strong> Not solid, exceptional: results with your fingerprints on the decision. A generically solid resume, or wins you can&#39;t explain, won&#39;t clear the bar.</p>

<p><strong>Obsessed with AI, to the point of building.</strong> You use AI daily, have built with LLMs, and reach for AI before asking a person when you need data or want to read code. You have real intuition for whether an AI experience is good, and a view on where it&#39;s headed. Unlikely to be a good fit if you treat LLM quality as a vibe check, or evals as engineering&#39;s problem.</p>

<p><strong>Obsessed with the customer and the Pro.</strong> You live in transcripts and Pro feedback to understand both sides, and hold their interests at once, with a rationale you&#39;d defend to either, neither indecisive nor a hip-shooter. Skip this one if you think you already know what our customers and Pros need.</p>

<p><strong>Ruthlessly data-driven.</strong> You find root causes through data, and reach for the number before the opinion. What doesn&#39;t work: leading with your gut, or refusing to move until the data is perfect.</p>

<p><strong>Unbelievable speed, without dropping quality.</strong> You ship scrappy, compress timelines, and it still makes you proud. You dive into unglamorous details like labeling eval sets and rewriting policy, because that&#39;s what quality costs. This won&#39;t work if perfectionism keeps you from shipping, or your speed leaves a mess behind.</p>

<p><strong>A thought partner who multiplies the room.</strong> You change leadership&#39;s mind, raise the bar for PMs by doing the work, and influence peers who don&#39;t report to you. Unlikely to be a good fit if you wait for direction, or guard what you know.</p>

<p><strong>This Role Is NOT</strong></p>

<ul>
<li><strong>A people-management role.</strong> You&#39;re an individual contributor with no direct reports. Influence comes from judgment and output, not a team you manage.</li>
<li><strong>A support PM role.</strong> Support is one surface of a broader area covering trust and AI, not a ticket queue or a help center.</li>
<li><strong>The internal-AI tooling role.</strong> A separate effort drives AI adoption in internal tooling. This role is about AI in the experience customers and Pros have.</li>
<li><strong>A strategy-only seat.</strong> You&#39;ll write evals, read transcripts, and prototype with LLMs. If you&#39;d rather just set direction, this will frustrate you.</li>
<li><strong>A tactical delivery role.</strong> This one owns where the service experience goes, not the next feature in it. If you&#39;re strongest turning someone else&#39;s strategy into tickets, this isn&#39;t the fit.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Benefits</strong></p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Base salary</strong>: $185,000 to $230,000</li>
<li><strong>Equity</strong>: We want our product managers to benefit from significant company growth, so equity is a meaningful part of how we structure PM compensation.</li>
<li><strong>Healthcare</strong>: Medical, dental, and vision</li>
<li><strong>Fully remote (US)</strong>: Deep thinking time and partnership with peers in engineering, design, data, marketing, and ops matter more than a desk.</li>
<li><strong>Flexible PTO</strong>: We focus on results. Take what you need.</li>
</ul>
